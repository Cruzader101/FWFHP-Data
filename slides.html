00 Study context

A clinic census, measured against a national yardstick

The FWFHP screens migrant agricultural workers at mobile night‑camp clinics using the PHQ‑2 — a two‑item depression screen scored 0–6, positive at a score > 2.

  • Unified 2024–2026 export; 1,896 responders carry a valid PHQ‑2 score.
  • The clinic is a convenience census, not a probability sample; NHANES (a national probability survey) is used as the reference.
  • NHANES only screens adults (18+); both cohorts are age‑matched to 18+ for the prevalence comparison.

01 Prevalence vs NHANES

PHQ‑2 positive prevalence — FWFHP vs NHANES

3.2%vs 13.9% · NHANES weighted
  • OR 0.21 (95% CI 0.16–0.28), z = −10.8, p ≈ 5.7×10⁻²⁷.
  • Survey weighting shifted the NHANES estimate 15.6% → 13.9%.
  • FWFHP n = 1,889 adults (61 positive); NHANES n = 912 unweighted.
Method · design‑aware comparison

NHANES point estimate uses the exam weight WTMEC2YR; its SE is Taylor‑linearized over 15 strata / 30 PSUs (cross‑checked against a delete‑one‑PSU jackknife). The OR and z‑test combine FWFHP’s binomial logit variance with the NHANES design SE via the delta method.

02 Predictors of a positive screen

Predictors of PHQ‑2 positivity — logistic regression

  • Male vs female: OR 0.33 (0.17–0.67), p = 0.002 — ~67% lower odds of screening positive.
  • Age (OR 1.00) and positive drug screen (OR 0.86) are flat.
  • No camp reaches significance; JR Baker is borderline (p = 0.07).
Method · logistic regression

PHQ2 ~ C(camp) + age + gender + drug_screen on 1,827 adults. Camps are dummy‑encoded (ref = Garcia Farms). Levels with a single outcome (e.g. 3R Harvesting, all‑negative) trigger quasi‑complete separation and are dropped dynamically so the model converges. McFadden pseudo‑R² = 0.057.

03 Screen‑positive → diagnosis

2 of 61 positive screens received a formal depression diagnosis

Formally diagnosed · of 61 positive 2 / 61 All 61 PHQ‑2 positive patients received further diagnostic assessment; 2 received a formal MDD/GAD diagnosis (3.3%).
  • Formal‑diagnosis rate 2 / 61 = 3.3% (95% CI 0.4%–11.3%, Clopper–Pearson).
  • The other 59 were assessed but did not receive a formal depression diagnosis.
Method · exact binomial CI

With 2 events in 61, the normal approximation breaks down. The Clopper–Pearson exact interval (proportion_confint(method='beta')) inverts the Beta distribution for honest coverage at tiny proportions.

04 Co‑occurrence — the power fix

Co‑occurrence — binary split vs continuous score

The binary test asked “is this diagnosis commoner in the PHQ‑2 > 2 bucket?” — collapsing the 0–6 scale (a 3 and a 6 counted the same) into a positive group of just 61. Everything came back ns after FDR.

  • Option A lines everyone up by score and asks: does risk climb steadily as the score rises?
  • Recovers power by using the full sample: n 61 → 1,896.
  • Re‑ranks signals by gradient, not top‑bin contrast.
Method · continuous predictor

Per diagnosis: Dx ~ a_phq2_score logistic fit; exp(β) is the OR per 1‑point rise. BH–FDR across the set. This is the graded ladder the analysis was meant to test.

04 Dose‑response results

Odds ratio per PHQ‑2 point, by diagnosis (BH‑FDR)

  • Dehydration · OR 1.69/pt (1.20–2.36), q = 0.021 — the only signal past FDR.
  • Headache trends the same (OR 1.53, raw p = 0.02) but q = 0.10 — suggestive only.
  • Elevated BP — the strongest binary signal — is flat here (OR 1.15, ns).
  • Eye Pain, Obesity, STIs (1–2 events) don’t converge — flagged, not reported.
Method · FDR + separation guard

Benjamini–Hochberg controls the false‑discovery rate across diagnoses. Near‑zero‑cell diagnoses cause separation; they’re caught and excluded rather than shown as spurious 0/∞ ORs. Firth penalized logistic is the noted follow‑up fix.

§ Methods at a glance

The statistical toolkit — and why each piece is here

Odds ratio + delta method

Combines two independent logits into a design‑aware OR; the delta method propagates the survey SE into the CI.

exp(log‑OR ± 1.96·SE)

Survey weighting

NHANES is a complex multistage sample; the exam weight + Taylor‑linearized variance give a defensible population estimate.

WTMEC2YR · SDMVSTRA / PSU

Logistic regression

Log‑odds model for predictors and for the graded co‑occurrence; dummy‑encoded categoricals, separation handled.

smf.logit(…)

Fisher’s exact test

The right 2×2 test when expected cell counts are tiny — used in the original binary co‑occurrence pass.

scipy.stats.fisher_exact

Benjamini–Hochberg FDR

Controls the false‑discovery rate across the diagnoses tested, rather than the family‑wise error rate.

multipletests(method='fdr_bh')

Clopper–Pearson CI

Exact binomial interval for very small proportions (2/61), where the normal approximation fails.

proportion_confint('beta')
› Caveats & next steps

Caveats & next steps

Caveats
  • Convenience vs probability sample. A benchmark, not a matched or randomized contrast — SES, language and access are uncontrolled.
  • PHQ‑2 is a screen, not a diagnosis. The low rate may reflect under‑reporting / stigma, plausibly the same reason so few are diagnosed.
  • Cross‑year double‑counting. IDs differ each year, so a patient seen twice is counted twice — violates independence; no de‑dup key.
  • Tiny event counts. 61 positives and several <15‑event diagnoses — an underpowered null, not evidence of no effect.
Next
  • Firth penalized logistic (Option B) for the zero‑cell diagnoses that won’t converge — finite estimates for Eye Pain / Obesity / STIs.
  • Clinical composites (e.g. any heat‑related = dehydration + heat rash + sunburn) — more power, fewer tests, groupings pre‑specified.
  • Bayesian shrinkage — weakly‑informative priors handle separation and give credible intervals on the 2/61 rate.
